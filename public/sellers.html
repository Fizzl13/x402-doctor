Selling through Cloudflare or Stripe

Cloudflare's Monetization Gateway (closed beta, US sellers and buyers) and Stripe's x402 machine payments (US businesses; elsewhere on request) both send a standard x402 v2 challenge and settle through the Coinbase CDP facilitator, so the free check above works on them as is. What to watch:

Cloudflare Monetization Gateway

  • No 402? A rule charges GET only until you select other methods, and its URL has to match the path you check. With the audience Verified Bots, only bots Cloudflare identifies are charged: other agents, and the check above, get the resource without paying. Choose Everyone to charge every caller.
  • Price between $0.001 and $100 per request. For a variable price (upto), your origin reports the actual amount in PAYMENT-SETTLEMENT on a successful response, and sets nothing on an error.
  • Verify PAYMENT-CONTEXT at your origin (an Ed25519 JWT, keys at https://payments.cloudflare.com/certs) and refuse requests without it, or anyone who reaches your origin directly gets the content for free.
  • Before a big buyer relies on you, order a paid delivery check ($0.25, on the home page): x402 Doctor buys your endpoint once and publishes whether it was charged and delivered.

Stripe x402

  • payTo is your Stripe crypto deposit address for that network, created once with the deposit addresses API: from live mode for Base mainnet, from a sandbox for Base Sepolia.
  • Mainnet settles through the CDP facilitator (your CDP API keys). Record each settled payment as a PaymentIntent in transaction_verification mode with the transaction hash.
  • Stripe records USDC on Base, Solana and Tempo: offer those networks in accepts.
  • To take cards from agents too, add MPP. The check above reads MPP challenges as well, Stripe's card method included.

What agents check before paying you

Agents using x402 Doctor's pre-payment check (GET /api/v1/preflight, also in x402-safe-fetch) get go, caution or no_go. You get caution or no_go when:

  • a payment would fail: wrong amount units, an unknown token, a missing Solana token account, a bad EIP-712 domain;
  • the price is higher than your OpenAPI says, or the page isn't HTTPS;
  • you were payable on fewer than half of your scanned days;
  • several buyers reported failed payments after paying you.

The five x402 attacks: what Doctor checks

The paper Five Attacks on x402 (May 2026) found five ways a paid endpoint can answer without being paid, or take a payment without answering. What x402 Doctor checks from outside, and what you fix in your own server:

  • Answering before the payment is final. An answer sent before settlement can lose its payment to a chain reorganisation. The paid delivery check measures the charge on chain next to the answer. Fix: settle before you answer (the x402 middleware does by default).
  • Someone else settles the payment first. An observer copies the payment header and submits it before your facilitator does: the buyer pays and gets nothing. This happens between your server and the facilitator, out of sight: settle over HTTPS through a facilitator you trust, and keep payment headers out of your logs.
  • One payment, many answers. The same payment header sent again must get a 402. x402 Doctor tests this on every Fizzl route each time it pays one. Fix: answer only after the facilitator has settled, so a used payment is refused.
  • A cache hands the paid answer out for free. The paid delivery check and the weekly spot-buy sample report the Cache-Control of the paid answer. Fix: send Cache-Control: no-store, private on every paid answer, so CDNs and proxies don't keep it.
  • Fake listings that steer agents. The Trust Index groups template farms into distinct services, flags lure listings and screens every payout address against the OFAC sanctions list; the pre-payment check turns that into go, caution or no_go.

Get found in the Bazaar

  1. Settle through the CDP facilitator (set your CDP API keys): resources that settle there (and declare discovery metadata) can be listed in the CDP Bazaar.
  2. Describe your resource: a clear description, input and output schema, serviceName, tags and an icon in your route config, and a /.well-known/x402.
  3. Stay payable: check yourself with step 1 after every deploy (or in CI: npx github:Fizzl13/x402-doctor <url> exits 1 on failures).
  4. Get attributed on Base: declare your Base Builder Code (minted at base.dev) with declareBuilderCodeExtension from @x402/extensions/builder-code. Step 1 tells you whether the facilitator will find it.
  5. Show it: the badge from step 2 on your README and site.
  6. Watch it: let the endpoint monitor (step 4) tell you on your phone, Telegram, Discord or Slack when it breaks, before your buyers notice.
